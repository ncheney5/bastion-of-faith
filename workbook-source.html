Part 1 of 6 · Alma 46:12

Govern

Teach correct principles first.

“And he wrote upon it—In memory of our God, our religion, and freedom, and our peace, our wives, and our children.”
Alma 46:12

Joseph Smith was once asked how he was able to govern so many people without it all falling into chaos. He answered, “I teach them correct principles, and they govern themselves.”

NIST: Set the direction: the values, roles, and policies everything else follows. At home: Write down family rules for devices, decide who owns which settings, and agree on what happens when a rule is broken.

Activity

Family Title of Liberty

  1. Gather the family with a large sheet of paper or poster board.
  2. Read Alma 46:12 together and talk about why Moroni wrote it down.
  3. Write what your home stands for and defends, in your own words.
  4. Have everyone sign it, then hang it where devices are charged so the “why” sits next to the screens.

Example

In our home we stand for

  • Jesus Christ and His gospel
  • Telling the truth, online and off
  • Treating our bodies and minds as sacred
  • Coming to Mom and Dad with anything

Signed: Dad · Mom · ________

Ponder and write

What correct principles do we want our children to carry into moments we can’t control?

If our family had a title of liberty, what would it say?

Part 2 of 6 · Alma 48:9

Identify

Find the weak places.

“And in their weakest fortifications he did place the greater number of men.”
Alma 48:9

Where is your home weak? Picture a fortress with only three walls. Where would you place most of your defenses? You would probably put most of your troops along the side with no wall. Would you put all of them there, though? No. You would keep some on the walls, because without them people would climb over and still get in.

NIST: Know what is worth protecting and how it could be attacked. At home: Make a list of every device on your network, every account your kids use, and which ones have the least protection.

Activity

Map the Fortress

  1. Draw your home as a fortress on a big sheet of paper.
  2. Add a gate for every way the world gets in: phones, games, apps, friends, school, TV.
  3. Talk about which gates are strongest and which are weakest. Mark the weak ones in red.
  4. Decide together where to put the most “troops,” and what one change you will make this week.

Ponder and write

Which gate into our home is weakest right now?

Where could something get in that we haven’t noticed yet, like the catacombs under the wall?

Part 3 of 6 · Alma 48:7

Protect

Prepare the minds of the people.

“Moroni, on the other hand, had been preparing the minds of the people to be faithful unto the Lord their God.”
Alma 48:7

This is where you patch the holes and make plans for when the fortifications fall. A plan should not be made during an attack. When the attack comes, your children should already know the plan and follow it. This is when you set it up.

NIST: Put safeguards in place before an attack comes. At home: Turn on content filtering, keep devices updated, use strong passwords and two-factor sign-in, and keep devices out of bedrooms at night.

Activity

Mind Fire Drill

  1. Explain that a fire drill works because you practice before there is a fire.
  2. Act out a few scenarios: a pop-up, a link from a friend, a video that turns bad.
  3. Each time, practice the same three steps: close it, walk away, tell a parent.
  4. Run the drill again in a month, just like a real fire drill.

Drill card

A video turns bad

  1. Close it. Hit the home button or close the lid.
  2. Walk away. Leave the room.
  3. Tell a parent. Right away, every time.

Practiced on: ________

Ponder and write

What is our family’s plan when something bad shows up on a screen?

Has everyone in the family practiced it out loud?

Part 4 of 6 · Alma 50:4

Detect

Know what ordinary looks like.

“He caused towers to be erected that overlooked those works of pickets.”
Alma 50:4

To detect something out of the ordinary, you must first know what ordinary is. Part of this is knowing what the companionship of the Spirit feels like, and what it feels like when the Spirit leaves. You also need a routine, and to know how you normally act, so you can tell when something is different. It is the difference between normal activity and odd behavior.

NIST: Notice when something is wrong, as early as possible. At home: Watch for odd network activity: new devices, logins at strange hours, or traffic to sites your family doesn’t use.

Activity

Spot the Difference

  1. Show two nearly identical pictures and have the family find what changed.
  2. Ask, “How did you know?” You could only spot it because you knew the original.
  3. Talk about what it feels like when the Spirit is with you, and when it leaves.
  4. Ask: what in our home has slowly become “normal” that shouldn’t be?

Circle 5 differences

Ponder and write

What does it feel like when the Spirit is with me?

What has slowly become normal in my life that shouldn’t be?

Part 5 of 6 · Genesis 39:12

Respond

Leave your garment and flee.

“And he left his garment in her hand, and fled, and got him out.”
Genesis 39:12

An action plan was set earlier. Now that the attack has been detected, this is where you act. You remove the influences that need to go, and you remove yourself from the threat.

NIST: Act on a detected attack to contain it. At home: Disconnect a compromised device, change passwords, block the source, and report scams.

Activity

Joseph’s Run

  1. Read Genesis 39:12 and talk about how fast Joseph left.
  2. Make scenario cards, like “A friend shows you something on their phone.”
  3. Take turns drawing a card and acting out how to flee.
  4. Make the family rule clear: telling a parent is always rewarded, never punished.

Scenario cards

A friend shows you something on their phone that you know is wrong.
A message says you won a prize and asks for Mom’s password.
A game chat starts asking where you live.

Ponder and write

What will I do in the first ten seconds?

Who will I tell, and how will they respond?

Part 6 of 6 · Alma 50:1; D&C 58:42

Recover

Rebuild the wall stronger.

“Moroni did not stop making preparations. … He who has repented of his sins, the same is forgiven, and I, the Lord, remember them no more.”
Alma 50:1; D&C 58:42

Repair the damage, and repent if need be. Then update the plan. Where did it fall short, and how can it be better in the future? What walls need to be rebuilt?

NIST: Restore what was damaged and improve for next time. At home: Restore from backups, review what happened, and close the hole that let the attack in.

Activity

Rebuild the Wall

  1. After any incident, hold a short, calm family review.
  2. Ask: What worked? Where did the plan fall short? What wall needs rebuilding?
  3. Write down one change to the plan.
  4. End by testifying that repentance makes the wall stronger, not weaker.

Family review

What workedWe told Dad right away.
Where the plan fell shortThe tablet had no filter on the browser app.
Wall to rebuildTurn on filtering for every app, not just the main browser.

Ponder and write

What did we learn the last time something got through?

What one wall will we rebuild this month?
